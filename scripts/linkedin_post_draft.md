# LinkedIn Post: The SOC 2 comfort blanket is putting your company at risk.

Most executive boards, risk officers, and internal auditors are reading vendor SOC 2 reports completely backwards. 

They collect the PDF, see an "unmodified" (clean) opinion in Section I, tick a GRC box, and assume their customer data is safe. 

It’s a massive governance mistake. Here is why:

❌ **Myth 1: "Our vendor is SOC 2 Certified."**
There is no such thing. It’s an attestation *opinion* on a highly customized, vendor-defined system boundary. If they carved their cloud hosting out of the scope, that "clean" opinion is practically useless.

❌ **Myth 2: "If Section I is clean, there are no failures."**
An auditor can issue a clean overall opinion even if the vendor had multiple control failures during the testing period. You have to jump to **Section IV** and hunt for exceptions (e.g., terminated employees keeping active access for 18 days).

⚠️ **The Real Trap: CUECs (Complementary User Entity Controls)**
A vendor's security is only valid if *your* team implements specific controls listed in the report (like enforcing MFA or quarterly user reviews). If a breach occurs because you neglected these, the vendor bears zero liability. You fell into the CUEC trap.
